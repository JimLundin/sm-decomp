# 05: Carry diagnosis through successive candidate retries

**What to build:** A failed increment drives an evidence-based next experiment and routine repair while keeping all candidates and the accepted predecessor recoverable. Diagnostic history follows retries across immutable runs, and three consecutive experiments without new discriminating evidence produce a concrete decision brief.

**Blocked by:** 03: Prepare and accept one supported routine/width increment.

**Status:** ready-for-agent

- [ ] Connect candidate failures to existing first-observable instruction/state/bus diagnostics and paired trace/snapshot evidence. Replay independently rebuilt accepted-predecessor and disabled-entry controls over identical verified inputs and intervals.
- [ ] Use passing controls to support increment attribution without claiming a specific root cause. A failing or incomplete control routes investigation to shared paths, prior assumptions, input, cache or harness; an original-core synchronization failure requires independent hardware/input/observer observations.
- [ ] Unexecuted changed keys route to covered intervals or actual removal/deferral. Panics, setup/build errors and budgets retain their stage/state/log evidence and remain error or incomplete outcomes.
- [ ] Record each hypothesis, predicted discriminator, experiment, result and linked new evidence. Carry ordered history and the consecutive-without-evidence count across candidate runs for the same unresolved increment; a retry or restart does not reset the streak, while new discriminating evidence does.
- [ ] Perform routine repairs within the selected increment without a human checkpoint, with a meaningful reproduction through the affected layer and fresh validation of the repaired candidate. Broad shared-hardware repairs remain separate work requiring refreshed readiness.
- [ ] Stop for a missing required input or decision beyond the agreed design. At the third consecutive experiment without new discriminating evidence, preserve state and present evidence-supported choices or a specific next experiment; escalation never advances acceptance.
- [ ] Retain failed run IDs, source snapshots/diffs, controls, experiment evidence and accepted predecessor lineage without rewriting terminal runs. The failure brief states the earliest disagreement, what controls establish and the exact input or decision needed.
- [ ] Verify transient-effect detection, both control-routing outcomes, evidence-streak continuity/reset and escalation across real immutable fixture runs. No failed, uncovered, incomplete or errored retry counts as an accepted increment.
